# Executive Brief – FX Hedging: Cloud Migration
**To:** Chief Financial Officer

**From:** Marianne Torricer

**Date:** November 12, 2026

**Subject:** Foreign Exchange Risk and Hedging Analysis
## Foreign Exchange Exposure
Our company is a U.S.-based cloud-migration consultancy that is expecting to receive €5.3 million from a Belgian telecommunications company in 182 days. Since the payment will be made in euros, one of our main concerns is how much that payment will be worth in U.S. dollars when we receive it.
The biggest risk is that the euro could weaken against the U.S. dollar during those six months. Based on the indicative forward exchange rate of 1.1705, the payment would be worth $6,203,650 if we locked in that rate. However, if the euro drops to 1.10 by the time we receive the payment and we do not hedge, we would only receive $5,830,000. That is a difference of $373,650, which could have a significant impact on our company's cash flow and overall profitability.
## Hedging Alternatives
To help manage this risk, I will look at three different hedging strategies and compare how each one could protect our company.

**Forward Contract:** This allows us to lock in an exchange rate today for the payment we will receive in six months. The main advantage is that we would know exactly how much we will receive in U.S. dollars. However, if the euro strengthens, we would not be able to benefit from the higher exchange rate.

**Money-Market Hedge:** This involves borrowing euros now, converting them into U.S. dollars, and investing those dollars until the payment is received. This can also help us secure a predictable amount, but it requires borrowing money and depends on the interest rates available.

**Currency Put Option:** This gives us the option to exchange our euros at a predetermined rate without requiring us to do so. It would protect us if the euro weakens while still allowing us to benefit if it strengthens. The downside is that we would need to pay an upfront premium of $102,820.
## Assumptions and Hypothesis
For this analysis, I am assuming that the Belgian company will pay the full €5.3 million in 182 days and that our main priority is protecting the amount we receive in U.S. dollars. I will also need to research actual exchange rates and interest rates to make sure the financial model uses reliable information.
I expect the forward contract to provide the most predictable dollar proceeds because it allows us to lock in the exchange rate ahead of time. However, I also expect the currency put option to provide higher net proceeds if the euro strengthens enough to make up for the premium we paid. If the option does not perform better under those conditions, that would challenge my prediction. I will also compare the money-market hedge to see whether it provides better guaranteed proceeds.
